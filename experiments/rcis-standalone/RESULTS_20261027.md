## RCIS component evidence

- Joint ready closures **worsen** makespan against the same-priority insertion-based single-task scheduler by **1.395 percent mean paired gain** (negative favors single-task) with 254 wins / 8 ties / 450 losses. Holm-adjusted paired Wilcoxon p = **1.98e-6**; filename-derived grouped Holm p = **2.01e-5**. Neither the local reference certificate nor construction from scratch entails global makespan dominance.
- Gap insertion matters: full RCIS versus append-only RCIS has **+7.886 percent** paired gain, 569 wins / 121 ties / 22 losses; grouped Holm p = **1.85e-59**. Gap insertion itself is an existing standard technique; do not claim it as new.
- Including a common target in parent shortlists gives **+1.346 percent** gain versus the no-common-target ablation, 323 wins / 132 ties / 257 losses; grouped Holm p = **5.16e-5**.
- Mean SLR: RCIS **9.9287**, single-task RCIS **9.7797**. Mean runtime: **0.0413 s** versus **0.0360 s**, so the joint mechanism is slower and worsens makespan on average.
- Mean measured communication ratio: RCIS **0.6431**, single-task RCIS **0.7271**; mean utilization: **0.5350** versus **0.5145**. These descriptive differences do not rescue the adverse makespan ablation or establish causal superiority.

Source: `rcis-dev-complete-v1/summary.md`, Overall averages, Secondary metrics, Wilcoxon table.

## Statistical and validity boundaries

- All reported pairwise means here are from the full 712 common instances excluding invalid LSTD. The first 500-instance report is archived, not suppressed.
- RCIS versus PEFT: +0.879 percent paired gain (Holm p=5.06e-5; filename-derived grouped Holm p=0.000435). Versus MPEFT +0.136 percent with Holm p=0.0931, **not significant**. Versus IPEFT -1.115 percent and LEFT -1.497 percent: overall paired Holm p=0.0251, but grouped Holm p=0.117 and 0.105 respectively, so those two do not survive both sensitivity criteria.
- Per-DAG groups are derived from filename fields and have not been verified as independent graph structures. The Friedman/Nemenyi and Wilcoxon results are **exploratory** and do not establish publication-ready confirmatory evidence.
- Repository-defined speedup and efficiency values sometimes exceed 1 in the averaged efficiency column. Investigate the definition/reference baseline before interpreting this metric or putting it into a manuscript.
- Only the development split was used; no untouched test-split experiments were run. The private manuscript repository and public `main` branch were not modified.
- This is evidence about the specific bounded RCIS variant and registered implementations, not a proof that all joint-closure methods are inferior, nor a claim about canonical HEFT/PEFT fidelity.

## Research implication

**Do not report RCIS joint placement as a successful makespan contribution yet.** A more defensible next iteration should address *when* to commit a join group. One plausible, untested hypothesis is that immediate closure consumes processor capacity too early and harms independent ready tasks, despite reducing communication-ratio measurements. Test this mechanism using exact decision traces and counterexamples before adding tunable parameters or running more corpus-wide searches.
